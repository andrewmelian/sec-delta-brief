Ball Corporation
Q2 2026 filing delta

What changed between Ball Corporation’s quarter ended June 30, 2026 and its prior-quarter filing, based only on the company’s SEC disclosures.

New filing: Form 10‑Q filed August 4, 2026 — SEC source

Prior filing: Form 10‑Q filed May 5, 2026 — SEC source

Prepared: August 18, 2026

One-minute read: Q2 sales and earnings advanced, but the mix matters. North and Central America produced much higher sales without higher comparable segment earnings year over year; South America supplied the strongest disclosed earnings expansion. Operating cash use improved sequentially as working-capital outflow eased, while repurchases accelerated. Ball added explicit tariff-contingency costs to its consolidation charges and continued to flag unpredictable aluminum input prices.

Q2 year-over-year sales rose 19.7% ($3.997B versus $3.338B), while attributable net earnings rose 4.2% ($221M versus $212M). The filing says higher raw-material costs—driven by aluminum prices and higher volumes—added $506M to Q2 cost of sales versus the prior-year quarter.

The disclosure points to higher North American costs, including operating and plant start-up costs, partly offset by favorable price/mix. EMEA includes the acquired Benepack business; Ball acquired 80% of two European beverage-can facilities in January 2026.

Cash conversion

Operating cash use improved sharply during Q2, but first-half working capital remained a major use of cash

First-half cash used in operating activities was $169M versus $777M used in Q1 alone, implying approximately $608M of operating cash generation during Q2. Ball attributed first-half operating cash use primarily to a $1.01B working-capital outflow, improved from the $1.15B outflow disclosed after Q1. This Q2 figure is a calculation from the two filings, not a separately labeled company KPI.

Capital allocation

Repurchases accelerated

Ball disclosed 1,783,620 shares repurchased during Q2, compared with 19,752 shares during Q1. The remaining authorization at June 30 was approximately $2.823B under the board-approved program running through 2027. The first-half cash-flow statement shows $115M spent on treasury stock acquisitions.

New or sharpened disclosure

Tariff contingencies appeared in restructuring-related charges

Q2 business-consolidation and other activities were a $22M charge, versus $12M in Q2 2025. Ball said 2026 charges included expenses associated with tariff contingencies for which it is seeking recovery, plus previously announced facility-closure costs. This tariff-contingency wording was not part of the corresponding Q1 charge description.

Risk watch

No formal Item 1A change, but aluminum-price uncertainty stayed prominent

Ball reported no Item 1A risk-factor changes for Q2. In MD&A, however, it again emphasized rising aluminum input prices and said it could not predict their effects through pass-through provisions and hedging. The filing also reported cost of sales at 83% of net sales in Q2 2026 versus 81% in Q2 2025.

Questions for management

Five source-grounded follow-ups

  1. How much of North America’s 24.4% sales increase came from metal pass-through versus underlying volume and mix?
  2. When should plant start-up costs stop weighing on North American comparable operating earnings?
  3. How much of EMEA’s Q2 sales and earnings growth was attributable to Benepack rather than organic operations?
  4. What portion of tariff-contingency costs has contractual recovery coverage, and on what expected timing?
  5. Does the roughly $608M implied Q2 operating cash generation change the cadence of the stated 2026 capital-return plan?
